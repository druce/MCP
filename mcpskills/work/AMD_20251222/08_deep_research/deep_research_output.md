## **2. EXTENDED PROFILE**

### **History and Origin Story**

Advanced Micro Devices was founded May 1, 1969, in Sunnyvale, California by Jerry Sanders and seven colleagues from Fairchild Semiconductor. The company started as a second-source manufacturer of logic chips, releasing its first product (Am9300 shift register) in 1970 and going public in 1972. Early growth came from second-sourcing Intel chips under an IBM agreement secured in 1982 for the 8088 processor.

**Key Historical Milestones:**

- **1975:** Diversified into RAM chips and logic counters with military-grade reliability focus
- **1991:** Launched Am386 microprocessor, triggering legal battle with Intel that reached the U.S. Supreme Court (resolved in AMD's favor in 1994)
- **1996:** Acquired NexGen for $857M to enhance processor designs
- **2000:** Released Athlon 1-GHz processor, marking entry into high-performance computing
- **2003:** Launched Opteron server chip, beating Intel to market with x86-64 architecture
- **2006:** Acquired ATI Technologies for $5.4B to gain GPU capabilities
- **2009:** Spun off manufacturing into GlobalFoundries, adopting fabless model to reduce capital intensity
- **2013:** Won custom chip designs for PlayStation 4 and Xbox One, establishing console foothold
- **2014:** Dr. Lisa Su appointed CEO, initiating strategic refocus on high-performance computing
- **2017:** Launched Ryzen processors on revolutionary Zen architecture with chiplet design, marking AMD's competitive resurgence against Intel

The company evolved from a chip second-sourcer into a diversified leader in CPUs, GPUs, and APUs (accelerated processing units), with the fabless transformation enabling capital-efficient innovation focus.

### **Core Business**

AMD operates in three primary segments:

1. **Data Center** - EPYC server processors, Instinct AI accelerators, adaptive computing solutions
2. **Client and Gaming** - Ryzen desktop/mobile CPUs, Radeon discrete GPUs, semi-custom console processors
3. **Embedded** - Embedded processors, FPGAs, adaptive SoCs for automotive, industrial, aerospace applications

The company designs high-performance x86 CPUs, GPUs, and heterogeneous computing solutions while outsourcing manufacturing primarily to TSMC. Revenue reached $32.0B annually with 10.3% net margin.

Advanced Micro Devices occupies a **strengthening but vulnerable competitive position** in the global semiconductor industry. The company has successfully transformed from a struggling Intel follower to a credible competitor across CPUs, GPUs, and AI accelerators under Lisa Su's decade-long leadership. With $32B revenue, 10.3% net margin, and strong free cash flow generation (~$5B annually), AMD possesses financial flexibility to invest in R&D ($6-7B) and pursue strategic initiatives. Client and embedded segments provide diversification but face mature market dynamics and cyclical headwinds.

**Overall assessment:** AMD is a **well-managed, technologically competitive semiconductor company** trading at a **premium valuation that fully reflects optimistic AI share gain assumptions**. The stock represents a high-beta play on data center AI infrastructure spending with significant execution and competitive risk.
