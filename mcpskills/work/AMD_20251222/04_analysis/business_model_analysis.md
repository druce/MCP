# AMD Business Model Analysis

## Core Business & Products/Services

AMD operates a **fabless semiconductor business model**, focusing on design and development while outsourcing manufacturing to third-party foundries like TSMC[1]. This approach allows the company to concentrate resources on innovation and R&D rather than capital-intensive manufacturing operations.

AMD's product portfolio spans four main segments:

**Data Center** – The primary growth engine, including AI accelerators, server CPUs (EPYC), GPUs (Instinct), FPGAs, SmartNICs, and Adaptive SoCs for data centers[1]

**Client** – CPUs and APUs for desktop and notebook PCs, primarily the Ryzen processor line[1]

**Gaming** – Discrete GPUs (Radeon) and semi-custom System-on-Chips (SoCs) for gaming consoles[1]

**Embedded** – Embedded CPUs, GPUs, APUs, FPGAs, and Adaptive SoC products for various embedded system applications[1]

## Revenue Streams

AMD generates revenue through multiple channels:

**Processors and Graphics Cards** – Primary revenue from selling CPUs (Ryzen, EPYC) and GPUs (Radeon, Instinct) to PC manufacturers, retailers, and data centers[1]

**Semi-custom Solutions** – Custom System-on-Chips for clients, notably gaming consoles from Sony PlayStation and Microsoft Xbox[1]

**Intellectual Property Licensing** – Licensing technology and IP to other companies[1]

**Data Center Products** – A rapidly growing segment driven by high-performance CPUs and GPUs for AI and High-Performance Computing[1]
